About Me

I am a psycholinguist interested in how people comprehend, learn, and remember language. My research focuses on the cognitive mechanisms underlying language processing, with a particular emphasis on how prior knowledge and expectations shape comprehension and memory.

I investigate how people generate predictions about upcoming language, how they revise these predictions when encountering unexpected information, and how these processes change across the lifespan. More broadly, I am interested in the relationship between language processing, learning, and memory, and in the cognitive mechanisms that support these processes.

To address these questions, I use experimental methods from cognitive psychology and psycholinguistics, including behavioral experiments, eye-tracking, and online studies.

From 2022 to 2026, I was Principal Investigator of Project A5, The Role of Language Experience and Surprisal in Learning and Memory, within the Collaborative Research Center Information Density and Linguistic Encoding at Saarland University. Previously, I held research and teaching positions in the Departments of Psychology and Computational Linguistics at Saarland University, as well as a postdoctoral position at Ruhr University Bochum.

I obtained my PhD from McGill University in Montreal, Canada, where I worked at the Center for Research on Brain, Language and Music. Before that, I studied Clinical Linguistics and Speech-Language Therapy at Philipps University Marburg and English and American Studies at the University of Bayreuth.
